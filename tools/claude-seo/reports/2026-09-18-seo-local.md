## 2. Google Business Profile to-do list for Melissa

**Honesty check first:** proximity is the single largest local-pack ranking input (~55% of ranking variance per the skill's cited research), and the current Helensville 3-pack is occupied by three salons physically inside Helensville with 23–66 reviews each. A salon 15 minutes away in Kaukapakapa, with 1 review, is not going to unseat that pack through GBP optimization alone. The list below is ordered by realistic impact on **what this business can actually move**: the Kaukapakapa pack (where it likely has little competition), "near me" mobile results triggered from Helensville-area phones, and the existing organic position (#6) for "hairdresser helensville" — not a guarantee of Helensville 3-pack entry.

1. **Reviews — highest leverage, start this week.** GBP shows 5.0★ from 1 review. Review count and recency are cited as a top-tier ranking input, and the site already has the right link ready (`https://share.google/8GVUyoirDJwTIXnxO`). Concrete action: after every appointment, send this text (SMS or WhatsApp) to every client, regardless of how the appointment went — sending it only to happy clients is Google's prohibited "review gating":
   > "Hi [Name], thanks for coming in today! If you have a minute, a quick Google review helps a lot for a small local salon: https://share.google/8GVUyoirDJwTIXnxO — Melissa"
   Also print the same link as a QR code on a small card at the reception/mirror area. Target: consistent trickle of reviews rather than a one-off burst, so the listing doesn't go quiet for 3+ weeks at a time.

2. **Confirm primary + secondary GBP category (cannot verify via API — check in the GBP dashboard).** Primary category is the top-weighted local-pack factor. Confirm the primary category is literally "Hair Salon." Add secondary categories that match services actually offered from the site's own service list (Keratin treatment, balayage, highlights, permanent tint, toner, blow wave, cut & finish) — e.g. "Hairdresser," "Beauty Salon" as secondaries if not already set. Do not add a category for a service not actually offered (e.g. don't add "Hair Extension Technician" unless Melissa confirms she does extensions).

3. **Service area setting.** In the GBP dashboard's service-area section, add Helensville, Wainui, and Waitoki alongside Kaukapakapa — mirroring what the site's schema already declares in `areaServed`. This is what expands "near me" eligibility from Helensville-area phones without claiming a Helensville address.

4. **Services list with pricing.** Add/update the GBP Services tab with the same menu the site already publishes: Keratin treatment $180, Women's cut & finish $50, Partial foils $90, Half head highlights $140, Full head highlights $180, Permanent tint touch-up $90, Permanent tint all-over $140, Balayage $230, Toner $40, Blow wave $50. Have Melissa confirm these prices are still current before publishing — the project context notes an expired 2024 keratin promo is still live on the `/keratin` page, so pricing accuracy is a known open issue, not something to assume is current.

5. **Photos.** GBP listings with more photos get materially more direction requests. Concrete target (a reasonable minimum for a boutique salon, not a guaranteed stat): at least 10 exterior shots (signage, parking, street view so first-time Helensville visitors recognise the building), 10 interior shots (styling stations, waiting area), and 10+ real before/after work photos (balayage, keratin, colour) — ideally the same real work shown in the site's own gallery, refreshed monthly rather than uploaded once and left static.

6. **Attributes.** Turn on whatever is accurate: online booking link (point at `/booking`), appointment-required, and — since the site repeatedly promotes "free parking" as a differentiator for the Helensville drive — the matching free-parking attribute if accurate. Confirm accessibility and Wi-Fi attributes with Melissa rather than guessing; mark unknown until confirmed.

7. **GBP Posts.** Weekly posts tied to real services/results (not the stale keratin discount). Posts don't directly move rankings but trigger "Post Justifications" snippets in search, which is free extra real estate in results.

8. **Q&A seeding.** Have Melissa (or the business, via the owner account) pre-seed 3–5 questions Helensville searchers are likely to ask, answered by the owner: e.g. "Do you serve Helensville clients?", "How far is the drive from Helensville?", "Is there free parking?" — GBP Q&A content is increasingly a source AI assistants pull from.

9. **GBP website link strategy.** Per the skill's cited guidance, don't point the GBP website link at the site's single strongest organic page (which for this site is likely the homepage or the keratin post); that risks a documented suppression pattern (Sterling Sky). Point it at the homepage or a neutral landing page, not at whatever page currently ranks best.

## 3. Bing Places and Apple Maps

Both matter disproportionately here because ChatGPT/Copilot/Alexa source from Bing's index (not GBP directly), and iPhone users searching "near me" from Helensville will hit Apple Maps/Siri — and the project's own GSC data shows 73% of this site's clicks are already mobile.

### Bing Places for Business
1. Go to `bingplaces.com`, sign in with a Microsoft account, search for the exact NAP: "Hair By Melissa A, 12 Magnolia Lane, Kaukapakapa 0875" — Bing may have an unclaimed listing already (often auto-populated from data aggregators); claim it if found, otherwise add new.
2. Enter NAP exactly matching GBP: name, "12 Magnolia Lane, Kaukapakapa 0875, Auckland, New Zealand", phone "+64 27 479 9320" / "0274 799 320", category "Hair Salon," hours matching the confirmed GBP hours, website URL, and the same photo set used on GBP.
3. Verification method (phone, email, or another route) is presented by Bing at claim time — cannot be predicted here; follow whatever Bing offers during the claim flow.
4. Keep it synced going forward: any future NAP or hours change must be pushed to Bing Places too, not just GBP and the website.

### Apple Maps (Apple Business Connect)
1. Go to `businessconnect.apple.com`, sign in with an Apple ID, search for "Hair By Melissa A" at 12 Magnolia Lane, Kaukapakapa 0875; claim if it exists, otherwise register a new listing.
2. Apple verifies ownership by phone, text, or a physical postcard to the listed address — the exact method is chosen by Apple during the claim flow.
3. Enter identical NAP, hours, category, phone, and website as GBP/Bing.
4. Use Apple Business Connect's "Showcases" feature (its equivalent of GBP Posts) for service promos once set up.
5. Add the same photo set.

## 4. Claims on the site that contradict the confirmed GBP facts

**Real contradiction found:** The homepage displays testimonial cards with 5-star icon rows and named "clients" — "Sarah Johnson," "Rachel Thompson," "Jessica Miller" on the homepage; "Sarah K.," "Emma T." on the Helensville page — presented as ordinary customer reviews with no sourcing or disclosure. Example, verbatim from the live homepage HTML: a testimonial block reading "...treatment was a game-changer for my frizzy hair!" attributed to "- Sarah Johnson" next to a row of five yellow star icons.

This visually implies an established base of glowing reviews. The actual, confirmed GBP figure is **5.0★ from 1 review**. There is no schema `aggregateRating` making this claim (that was correctly removed at some point — good), but the visual testimonial cards on the page itself create the same false impression through content rather than markup. This is the one finding in this audit that should be treated as a genuine trust/compliance issue, not just an SEO gap: showing multiple named "customers" with star ratings when the business has one verifiable public review is the kind of pattern that review-authenticity rules (referenced in the skill as a Google fake-engagement policy and FTC concern) are aimed at. Recommend either (a) replacing these with real testimonials as they come in from the review-request campaign in §2, or (b) relabeling them clearly as illustrative/not verified Google reviews until real ones exist.

**Everything else checked was consistent, not contradictory:**
- Address (12 Magnolia Lane, Kaukapakapa 0875) matches GBP exactly on all 5 pages — the project's own context file's note about a wrong "0810" postal code does not reflect the current live site.
- Phone (0274 799 320 / +64274799320) matches GBP exactly on all 5 pages.
- Hours (Mon–Fri 9–1:30, Sat 2–5, Sun 9–5) match GBP exactly on all 5 pages — the context file's notes about inconsistent/wrong hours across the site do not reflect the current live site.
- The Helensville location page correctly says the salon is "located in nearby Kaukapakapa," not in Helensville itself — no false-location claim.
- Instagram handle in `sameAs` and footer matches the confirmed handle exactly.

**Minor point worth Melissa's awareness, not a contradiction:** the site's primary schema `name` is "Hair By Melissa" while GBP's verified name is "Hair By Melissa A" (the site does carry "Hair By Melissa A" correctly as a schema `alternateName`). This isn't wrong — Google appends disambiguation suffixes like this itself, and forcing the site's primary name to match exactly is not required or necessarily advisable. Flagging it only so that when Bing Places / Apple Maps listings are created, Melissa decides once whether to use "Hair By Melissa" or "Hair By Melissa A" and applies it consistently everywhere rather than picking ad hoc.

- **Not realistically achievable through website or GBP optimization alone:** ranking inside Helensville's own Google 3-pack against Chanelle Jade Hair Studio, Fifth Hair Studio, and Smart Cuts. All three are physically located in Helensville with more reviews; proximity is too large a ranking input to overcome from 15 minutes away.
- **Achievable and worth targeting:**
  - The Kaukapakapa local pack, where there's little evidence of competing salons.
  - "Near me" mobile pack/map results triggered from phones physically in or near Helensville, once review count, photos, and citation consistency (Bing/Apple) improve — proximity still helps here even without a Helensville address, because the trigger is the searcher's live location, not a fixed pack tied to "Helensville" as a query term.
  - The existing organic (blue-link) position #6 for "hairdresser helensville" — this is separate from the pack, already working, and worth protecting/improving through the location page content already in place rather than chasing pack entry.
